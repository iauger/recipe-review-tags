Structuring Recipe Reviews Through Qualitative Tagging Assignment¶

Author: Ian Auger

Abstract¶

Online recipe platforms suffer from "information density" and a severe optimism bias, where critical qualitative signals (taste issues, prep difficulty) are buried within millions of uniformly positive 5-star free-text reviews. This project develops a scalable multi-label tagging system using a Transformer-based Zero-Shot baseline to generate "Silver Labels" for training a distributed Word2Vec centroid classifier in PySpark. By establishing a custom 128-dimensional culinary vector space, we demonstrate an automated thresholding approach to reach a ~65,000-sample ground truth, successfully extracting minority instructional critiques from high-volume praise.

1. Introduction & Objectives¶

User-generated recipe reviews contain rich, multidimensional signals regarding taste, process, and ingredient reliability. However, these insights are embedded in unstructured free text. Traditional statistical measures like TF-IDF or Bag-of-Words (BOW) often fail to capture the semantic nuances such as negation or intensity that are required for high-level qualitative analysis.

The objective of this project is to develop a scalable multi-label tagging system that transforms raw review text into structured feedback. To achieve this, I employ a Transformer-based Zero-Shot baseline to generate a ground-truth dataset, which is then used to train a distributed Word2Vec-based centroid classifier in PySpark. This allows for the high-throughput labeling of over 1 million reviews while maintaining semantic accuracy.

1.1 Qualitative Taxonomy¶

The taxonomy consists of 17 tags across 6 thematic groups, designed to capture both "Success" and "Risk" signals.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)¶

Before applying any semantic models, I performed extensive EDA to understand the distribution of the Food.com review corpus and to establish a threshold for "Informative" content.

2.1 The "Informative Review" Filter¶

To ensure each review provides enough context for stable Word2Vec embeddings, I analyzed the distribution of token counts to establish a "Golden Range" of semantic density.

  • Informative Floor (15 tokens): Excludes short, low-signal reviews (e.g., "Thanks!") that lack the "Subject-Modifier" pairs required for thematic tagging.
  • Semantic Ceiling (120 tokens): The 95th percentile of the corpus sits at roughly 118 tokens. In a vector space where document vectors are derived by averaging, extreme length acts as a "semantic black hole," washing out specific, localized critiques (e.g., "too salty"). Implementing a ceiling at 120 tokens mitigates this vector dilution.

2.3 N-Gram Analysis & Preprocessing Justification¶

Frequency analysis of corpus bigrams revealed that top pairings (e.g., "i used," "it was") are dominated by high-frequency syntactic glue words.

  • Syntactic Noise: The top bigrams (e.g., "i used," "it was," "thanks for") are almost entirely composed of high-frequency "glue" words that carry zero culinary or process-related information.
  • Stop Word Strategy: Initial iterations preserved negation particles ("not") and hedging adverbs ("probably", "kind of") to contextualize adjectives. However, vector audits revealed these high-frequency tokens became "Semantic Hubs," pulling distinct culinary centroids together. Consequently, an aggressive, custom stop-word purge was implemented to force the model to map pure culinary intent.
  • Window Size Choice: To combat the loss of syntactic connectors, the Word2Vec window size was restricted to 3. This ensures the model learns tight noun-verb-adjective groupings (e.g., "fish was bland") without diluting token weight across distant, unrelated context.

3. Methodology: Semantic Tagging & Centroid Logic¶

The core of this tagging system is the transformation of unstructured review text into a dense, 128-dimensional vector space where semantic proximity correlates to culinary themes.

3.1 Foundational Vector Space: Word2Vec¶

Rather than treating tokens as isolated indices, I employ the Word2Vec paradigm to learn distributed representations from the entire review corpus. That is, the cleaned review text is vectorized so that it can be measured relationally against the average centroids from the transformer labeled dataset.

  • Semantic Context: This approach ensures that terms like "salty," "brine," and "sodium" are mathematically proximal, allowing the model to recognize "risk signals" even when specific keywords vary.
  • Dimensionality Compression (128-D): Rigorous experimentation revealed that expanding to 300 dimensions in a heavily imbalanced dataset induced the "Hubness Problem," where high-frequency adverbs became gravitational centers. Compressing the space to 128 dimensions forces the Word2Vec model to map true semantic intent rather than memorizing syntactic noise.
  • Hyper-Focused Context Window: The model utilizes a strict window size of 3. This hyper-local window captures immediate "Subject-Modifier" triplets (e.g., "sauce was salty") while preventing the topical bleed that occurs when unrelated ingredients from across a long review influence a single vector.

3.2 Label Centroid Construction¶

To assign tags, I define a "representative vector" or Centroid for each of the 15 qualitative categories in our taxonomy.

  • Mathematical Definition: A label centroid ($C_j$) is calculated as the mean vector of all high-confidence "Silver Label" review vectors assigned to that category by the Transformer baseline.
  • Hardware Constraints: This project did not have GPU resources, so the Transformer baseline was run locally on a CPU over multiple runs, limiting the population size of labeled data. Future work will look to expand the labeled dataset to 20% of the total review population to capture a diverse set of reviews and more accurately calculate the average label centroid.
  • Centroid formula: Based on the work of Mustafa et al. (2021), the average similarity score for a category is defined as:

$$AS_{c}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}SS_{C_{i}}(T_{p},P_{G_{i}})$$

  • Multi-Label Mapping: Unlike single-class systems that select the absolute maximum similarity, culinary feedback is inherently multi-label (e.g., a dish can be both easy_quick and too_salty). This system projects a new review vector into the space and independently calculates its cosine similarity against all 17 learned centroids to identify overlapping themes.

3.3 Rigorous Similarity Thresholding¶

A significant challenge in Multi-Label Classification (MLC) is determining the optimal cutoff for tag assignment without relying on arbitrary global thresholds (e.g., a flat 0.5).

  • Diagonal Extraction: I adopted a data-driven calibration approach by calculating a correlation matrix of average similarity scores across the 65,000-sample validation set. The unique threshold ($\tau$) for each tag is extracted from the diagonal of this matrix, representing the internal semantic consistency of that specific culinary theme. This ensures that high-variance tags (delicious_tasty) and highly specific tags (too_acidic) operate on their own calibrated scales.
  • Iterative Precision: This allowed for the thresholds to update automatically as the labeled data size grew and the model became more precise over multiple iterations, preventing the need to apply arbitrary threshold values or assume domain expertise.

4.2 Modular Breakdown & Data Lineage¶

The pipeline utilizes a modular "Lakehouse" pattern designed for distributed PySpark execution, ensuring data immutability and reproducibility.

  • Phase 1: Bronze (Ingestion): Raw interactions and recipes are validated and committed to immutable Parquet storage to preserve the original schema.
  • Phase 2: Silver (Cleaning & Density Filtering): The pipeline applies the 15-120 token density filter alongside aggressive regex normalization and targeted stop-word pruning. This phase strips "syntactic glue" to maximize the semantic signal-to-noise ratio for downstream vectorization.
  • Phase 3: Gold (Structuring & Calibration): A deterministic SHA-256 review_key anchors the dataset, allowing the pipeline to safely fork into two branches.
  • The Semantic Split: The supervised branch generates "Silver Standard" ground-truth labels via a Transformer model, while the unsupervised branch trains the global 128-D Word2Vec space.
  • Threshold Calibration: The thresholds.py module reconciles these branches, applying the Diagonal Extraction algorithm to produce a fully calibrated, production-ready semantic space.

5. Semantic Calibration & Results¶

This section evaluates the accuracy of the vector space and the effectiveness of the unsupervised thresholding strategy. By utilizing the full review corpus for Word2Vec training, the model achieves a high-resolution "culinary map" where qualitative critiques are mathematically distinct.

5.1 Centroid Proximity & Semantic Integrity¶

The core of the labeling system relies on the Diagonal Extraction method to identify the optimal cosine similarity cutoff for each of the 17 tags. Based on the framework established by Mustafa et al. (2021) , this data-driven approach extracts thresholds from the internal semantic consistency of each tag cluster , effectively adapting to the linguistic density of different culinary themes.

  • Automated Calibration: This approach removes the need for arbitrary global thresholds (e.g., a flat 0.5).
  • Addressing the Success Cluster: By calculating independent thresholds per tag, the model ensures that the high-prevalence "Success Cluster" (which accounts for ~94% of reviews) does not drown out the signal for rare "Risk" tags like too_acidic or ingredient_issue. Broad tags like family_hit organically settle at lower thresholds, while specific critiques demand higher similarity scores.
    • Vector Fidelity: High diagonal values (e.g., > 0.70) indicate that the 65,000-sample labeled subset has formed tight, mathematically coherent clusters within the compressed 128-dimension space, proving that the Window-3 context successfully captured specific culinary "triplets".

6. Multi-Label Inference¶

With the establishment of the compressed 128-dimension semantic space and the calibrated tag-specific thresholds, the next step is to apply this lens onto the entire 1.07 million review corpus by executing the inference pipeline.

Unlike the initial Zero-Shot labeling, which relied on external LLM heuristics, this phase is entirely localized and deterministic. Each review is projected into the custom Word2Vec space and compared against the 17 tag centroids using the semantic floors established previously. This allows the reviews to be efficiently assigned relevant tags without the latency or computational overhead of a traditional LLM.

6.1 The Inference Architecture¶

The inference process follows three distinct stepss to transform raw text in to binary labels:

  1. Vectorization: The prep_model and w2v_model transofrm the cleaned reviews into 128-dimensional dense vectors.
  2. Proximity Calculation: For reach review vector $V_r$, the cosine similarity $S$ is calculated to each of the 17 tag centroids $C_i$
  3. Boolean Thresholding: A label $Y_I$ is assigned if $S({V_r},{C_i}) >= {T_i}$

6.2 Label Distribution Analysis¶

To verify the balance and separability of the tags across the full corpus, I analyzed the final label prevalence. Given the established class imbalance within the dataset (the "Success Cluster" ), the primary concern during inference is the over-assignment of positive tags at the expense of neutral or negative critiques, which are often more instructive to users.

8. Conclusion¶

The v8 Hardened Model represents a significant architectural leap over generic sentiment analysis. By constructing a custom 300-D Word2Vec space on 1.07 million domain-specific reviews, we have created a "Culinary Intelligence" layer that prioritizes Precision and Separability.

The final "Gold" dataset provides a high-fidelity mapping of recipe intent, allowing future users to bypass the 5-star "Optimism Bias" and find the specific, instructional critiques that lead to better cooking outcomes.

8.1 Future Work: Aspect-Based Sentiment¶

While the V1 Word2Vec architecture successfully mapped the culinary domain, pure statistical co-occurrence models face inherent limitations regarding grammatical intent. During testing, we encountered the "Semantic Neutralization" problem, where high-frequency hedging adverbs (e.g., "probably", "kind of") and negation particles ("not") acted as gravitational hubs in higher-dimensional vector spaces (300-D), distorting the proximity of true culinary modifiers.

To resolve this and elevate the model to an enterprise-grade recommendation engine, future iterations of this pipeline will implement Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis via Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging.

Proposed V2 Architecture:

  • Syntactic Filtering: Integrating a distributed NLP library (such as Spark NLP) to parse sentence dependency trees. By isolating Noun-Adjective pairs (chicken $\rightarrow$ dry, sauce $\rightarrow$ salty) and discarding non-instructive adverbs prior to Word2Vec ingestion, we can achieve absolute semantic density.

  • Vocabulary Splitting: Appending POS tags directly to tokens (e.g., salt_NOUN vs. salt_VERB) to prevent multi-use culinary words from collapsing into single, ambiguous centroids.
